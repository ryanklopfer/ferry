# US out-of-network behavioral-health market: structural dynamics and software competitive landscape (as of October 2026)

Scope note: US only. Dates are given for every figure. Anything from 2022 or earlier is flagged "(pre-2023)". Mentaya, Reimbursify, Thrizer and Twofold are covered only for fresh public facts (no SWOT). "Undisclosed" means no verifiable public figure was found. Aggregator sites (Tracxn, CB Insights, Sacra, Caplight, Forge, Latka) frequently conflict; where they do, the conflict is shown rather than resolved.

## KQ1. Is the number of OON / private-pay clinicians growing or shrinking?

### Takeaway
The best longitudinal series (APA, psychologists only) shows the in-network share falling from 66% to 62% between 2024 and 2025, with 10% of in-network psychologists dropping at least one commercial panel in the prior 12 months. The best cross-sectional count (Psychology Today scrape, Dec 2023, 175,083 therapists) puts the no-insurance share at about 35%. The one counter-signal is SimplePractice's own 2025 report, which says the share of sessions billed to insurance among its users rose from 56% to 59% (2024 to 2025). Net: the OON pool is stable-to-slightly-growing in headcount while the in-network platforms (Headway, Grow, Alma/Spring, Rula, SonderMind) are growing much faster in absolute terms; the two coexist rather than one draining the other.

### Inferences
- Using the APA series as the only true year-over-year measure, the no-insurance share of psychologists moved from 34% (2024) to 38% (2025): a 4-point rise in one year. Psychologists are the highest-fee, most-OON profession (57% accept insurance vs. 66% for master's-level), so the OON pool skews toward psychologists and psychiatrists.
- Rough sizing: if ~35% of ~175k directory-listed therapists accept no insurance, the pure-OON private-practice pool is on the order of 60k clinicians (2023 base), before adding hybrid practices and psychiatrists. Headway alone claims 65-70k providers in 2026, so the in-network platforms have grown past the size of the OON pool in roughly five years without visibly shrinking it.
- The SimplePractice 56%→59% insurance-session figure and the APA 66%→62% in-network figure are not contradictory: platforms lower the cost of taking insurance for master's-level clinicians (who dominate SimplePractice), while psychologists continue to exit. The OON segment is becoming more concentrated in higher-fee, doctoral-level and psychiatric practices.
- Headway's new $9.99 client-side fee and the Optum cuts are the kind of events that push marginal platform clinicians toward hybrid/OON models; APA's 10% annual commercial-panel exit rate is the measurable churn Ferry would recruit from.

### Gaps
- No APA series before 2024 for network participation (survey was redesigned after May 2023), so a 2020-2025 trend is unavailable.
- No time series for Psychology Today "accepts insurance" shares; only the single Dec 2023 scrape.
- Thrizer/Beacon report's split of fully-insurance vs. private-pay vs. hybrid practices (reported elsewhere as 8%/32%/60%) could not be verified from the PDF.
- Headway's 2025-2026 provider count is unverified; no Headway 2025 annual report was retrievable.
- Reddit (r/therapists, r/psychotherapy) sentiment could not be searched (reddit.com blocks the crawler); only trade-press summaries of therapist misgivings were available.

## KQ2. Demand side: utilization, telehealth share, wait times, payer behavior, parity rule status

### Takeaway
Demand is structurally up (BH utilization +62.6% 2018-2024; BH visits surpassed primary-care visits among the commercially insured in 2024), supply is short (27.3% national provider adequacy, 2025), telehealth has settled into a hybrid norm (65% of psychologists hybrid, 21% fully remote in 2025), and the federal 2024 parity rule is not being enforced and will be replaced; ghost-network findings from HHS-OIG (Oct 2025) keep directory accuracy on the enforcement agenda.

### Cited Findings
- Trilliant Health 2026 Behavioral Health Report (data 2018-2024): BH utilization up 62.6% to 1,346 visits per 1,000; anxiety-disorder utilization up 89.3%; BH share of all telehealth volume rose from 18.4% (2018) to 65.6% (2024); national mental-health-professional adequacy rate 27.3% in 2025 (WV 5.7% to NJ 52.3%); projected counseling FTE shortfall 99,780 by 2038; 83% of BH providers reported burnout (2023); commercial UHC negotiated psychotherapy rates vary up to 7x, with CPT 90837 median $147 (range $78-$542, 2025). — [Trilliant 2026 BH Report](https://www.trillianthealth.com/market-research/reports/2026-behavioral-health-report); [MedCity, Apr 2026](https://medcitynews.com/2026/04/behavioral-health-demand-trilliant/)
- BH visits (66.4M) exceeded primary-care visits (62.8M) among commercially insured in 2024. — [BHB, Oct 2025](https://bhbusiness.com/2025/10/09/behavioral-health-visits-eclipsed-primary-care-visits-in-2024/); [AHA, Nov 2025](https://www.aha.org/aha-center-health-innovation-market-scan/2025-11-11-behavioral-health-outpaces-primary-care-2024)
- Trilliant 2025 reporting: BH made up 67% of telehealth visits in 2024, but telehealth visit count fell to under 40M from nearly 50M in 2023 (telehealth plateauing). — [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/payers/trilliant-report-behavioral-health-utilization-telehealth-plateauing)
- APA 2025 modality series (psychologists): fully remote 64% (2020) → 47% (2021) → 31% (2022) → 21% (2023) → 19% (2024) → 21% (2025); hybrid 33% → 49% → 58% → 67% → 69% → 65%; all in-person 3% → 14%. ### Inferences
- Demand growth plus a 27% adequacy rate means OON clinicians face little pricing pressure from excess supply; the binding constraint for clients is affordability, which is exactly what OON reimbursement tooling addresses.
- With the 2024 parity rule shelved and the replacement unlikely to be in force before 2027, there is no near-term regulatory lever forcing payers to improve in-network BH access, which sustains OON demand.
- Telehealth has settled into a durable hybrid norm (about two-thirds of psychologists hybrid); recording-based scribes must handle both in-person and remote sessions, and a meaningful minority (14%) is in-person only.

### Gaps
- No authoritative share-of-BH-visits-delivered-via-telehealth series for 2025 (Trilliant gives BH share of telehealth, not telehealth share of BH).
- No reliable OON-specific denial-rate data for behavioral health; Virginia's 2024 state report (40.6% of MH claims paid) was only visible through a secondary blog.
- Outcome of the 2026 MHPAEA replacement rulemaking unknown as of this writing.

Consolidation is active at every layer above the solo practice: 180 BH provider deals in 2025 (Mertz Taggart), SimplePractice under Vista since Jan 2024, Spring Health absorbing Alma (May 2026), Grow absorbing an AI scribe (Feb 2026), Headway acqui-hiring AI talent (Mar 2026). ## KQ4. Reimbursement trends: are OON benefits shrinking? allowed amounts? denial rates?

### Takeaway
Plan mix is slowly moving away from PPOs (48% of covered workers in 2017 → 46% in 2025) toward HDHP/SOs (28% → 33%), but PPOs remain the plurality and most HDHPs still carry OON benefits after the deductible, so the addressable OON-benefit population is shrinking slowly, not collapsing. No credible data shows OON allowed amounts or BH-specific OON denial rates trending in either direction.

### Cited Findings
- KFF 2025 Employer Health Benefits Survey: PPO 46%, HDHP/SO 33%, HMO 12%, POS 9%, conventional <1% of covered workers; HDHP/SO enrollment higher at firms with 200+ workers (35%) than small firms (26%). — [KFF 2025 EHBS](https://www.kff.org/health-costs/2025-employer-health-benefits-survey/); [Summary PDF](https://files.kff.org/attachment/Employer-Health-Benefits-Survey-2025-Annual-Survey-Summary-of-Findings.pdf)

PPO: 48% (2017), 44% (2019), 47% (2020), 49% (2022), 47% (2023), 48% (2024), 46% (2025). — [KFF 2024 EHBS](https://www.kff.org/health-costs/2024-employer-health-benefits-survey/); [KFF 2023](https://www.kff.org/health-costs/2023-employer-health-benefits-survey/); [KFF 2020 summary](https://files.kff.org/attachment/Summary-of-Findings-Employer-Health-Benefits-2020.pdf); [KFF 2017 summary](https://files.kff.org/attachment/Summary-of-Findings-Employer-Health-Benefits-2017)

- General claim-denial trend: UnitedHealthcare ACA-marketplace denial rate fell from 34.2% to 19.1% (not BH-specific); Kodiak found hospital/physician initial denials fell vs. H1 2025 but takebacks rose 13.8%. — [MoneyGeek 2026](https://www.moneygeek.com/insurance/health/aca-claim-denial-rates-by-state-and-insurer/); [QuickIntell 2026](https://quickintell.com/insights/state-of-healthcare-denials-2026)

### Inferences
- The 2024→2025 HDHP jump (27%→33%) is the largest single-year move in the series; if it holds, more clients will be paying full fee until a deductible is met, which raises the value of accurate benefits estimation and claim tracking (deductible accumulation) rather than eliminating OON reimbursement.
- Because OON payment is pegged to UCR and plan coinsurance, the lever Ferry controls is claim completeness and persistence (resubmission, appeals), not the allowed amount.

## KQ9. Verdict: expanding, consolidating, or mature?

### Takeaway
The OON private-practice behavioral-health market is expanding on the demand side and stable-to-growing on the supply side, while the software layer around it is consolidating. Demand: BH utilization up 62.6% (2018-2024), BH visits now exceed primary-care visits for the commercially insured, provider adequacy sits at 27.3%, and the only federal lever that would force better in-network access (the 2024 parity rule) has been shelved and will be rewritten. Supply: the share of psychologists in-network fell from 66% to 62% in one year, 10% dropped a commercial panel in the prior 12 months, 75% cite reimbursement and 57% cite administrative burden as the reason, and about 35% of directory-listed therapists take no insurance at all, even as Headway (65-70k providers), Grow ($1B revenue, $3B valuation) and Spring/Alma (26k) have grown an in-network alternative larger than the OON pool itself; SimplePractice's user data (insurance sessions 56%→59%) shows the two coexist, with master's-level clinicians drifting in-network and doctoral/psychiatric clinicians drifting out. Reimbursement: PPO share is eroding slowly (48%→46%) and HDHP/SO jumped to 33% in 2025, which shrinks the OON-benefit population at the margin but increases the value of benefits estimation and claim persistence. Tooling: the scribe segment is mature in pricing ($19-$99/month, free tiers, per-session anchors) and consolidating by acquisition (Tenor into Grow, Tezi into Headway, SimplePractice under Vista, Alma into Spring), while the OON-reimbursement segment is small, unfunded and client-pays. Sub-segments that differ from the whole: psychologists and psychiatrists (highest fees, lowest insurance acceptance, most OON) are the expanding core; master's-level therapists are being pulled in-network by free platform tooling; California, DC and Tennessee are the most OON-heavy states but California also carries all-party consent law risk for recording. No competitor combines ambient notes with clinician-paid, client-payee OON filing and appeal letters as of October 2026.
