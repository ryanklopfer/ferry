# Mentaya — Reverse-Engineering & SWOT (2026-09-16)

## What Mentaya actually is

Mentaya (legal name Zaya Health Inc., San Francisco; Crunchbase: pre-seed, 1–10 employees, investors Pear VC and Banana Capital) is a **therapist-distributed, client-paid out-of-network claims concierge**. Founded by Christine Li (ex-Google PM on Google AI / YouTube); she is now listed as Founder & President / Chairman and Maria Lulo (Duke psychology) is CEO — a founder-to-operator handoff that happened quietly, with no announcement found. Customer Success is a named single lead. Positioning: "Therapists get their full cash rates. Clients get up to 80% back on therapy." Their own line vs. Thrizer/Reimbursify (Apr 2026 comparison post): the "done-for-you" option — free to the therapist, no forced payment-rail switch, claims guarantee, concierge team.

No scale metrics are published anywhere (no therapist count, claim count, or dollars reimbursed on the site, About page, help center, or in four podcast appearances). The only numbers they put out are outcome averages: clients get "~62% of therapy costs" back post-deductible, "40–80%" range, and the benefits checker resolves ~90% of checks automatically with concierge backup.

### Architecture (observed + inferred)
- Two products, priced separately:
  1. **Instant Benefits Checker** — $29/mo (annual) or $39/mo (monthly), unlimited checks, embeddable website widget. Inputs: client name, DOB, member ID; returns OON deductible status and estimated reimbursement against the practice's CPT/fee schedule. This is sold as a *marketing* tool ("convert more private-pay clients"), not a billing tool. 30-day free trials via podcast/partner codes (HEARD, TGPE).
  2. **Automated Claim Submission** — free to therapist; client pays 5% of the session fee per claim, first claim free. Therapist sets "Claim Defaults" once (diagnosis, CPT, place of service, duration, fee); after each session the therapist confirms it happened via SMS reply, app button, bulk submit, or weekly auto-submission, and Mentaya files the claim. Client does nothing after signup ("set it and forget it").
- Three charge modes: *Claim only* (therapist keeps their own EHR/processor; Mentaya just files and bills the client 5%), *Session + Claim* (Mentaya charges the client's card for the session via Stripe and adds 5%), *Session only*. Stripe fees are pass-through with no visible markup: 2.9% + 30¢ card, 0.8% ACH capped at $5. This is the structural answer to Thrizer: Mentaya does not need to own the payment rail.
- **Free EHR (beta)**: scheduling, notes, intake forms, billing, document management; Google Calendar / iCal sync; access by emailing support. No named third-party EHR integrations — Slashdot lists "No Integrations," and "works with your existing EHR" means coexistence via Claim-only mode, not data sync. No native mobile app.
- Reimbursement to client: mailed check with EOB (help center) or direct deposit (patient page); "as little as a week, up to 45 days." No float, no instant reimbursement, no coinsurance-only option at point of service.
- Denials/appeals/payer registration: "an entire team of insurance pros" resubmits and follows up at no extra cost. Guarantee wording is inconsistent — marketing says "Successful *reimbursement* – or your money back"; the help center and pricing article say the refund applies "if we don't successfully get your claim *submitted*/accepted." In practice it is a fee refund on rejected claims, not a reimbursement guarantee (Thrizer's comparison makes the same point).
- Payer scope: PPO, POS, HDHP only; explicitly not HMO, Medicaid, or traditional Medicare. Primarily mental health; help center says they are "interested in" SLP, PT, nutrition but do not market to them.
- Group practices: multiple admins, invite providers, move clients between providers, submit claims on behalf of any clinician, per-clinician fee schedules in the benefits checker. No per-seat pricing.
- Known gaps from the field: no support for add-on CPT codes (96131/96137 — kills testing psychologists' multi-hour evaluations); a community report that account cancellation was unavailable; a percentage fee that is charged on every session including those before the deductible is met.

### Unit economics (reconstructed)
For a $200 session: therapist pays $0 (or $6.10 Stripe pass-through if charging through Mentaya, none of which appears to be Mentaya margin); client pays $10 regardless of deductible status or eventual reimbursement. Full-time caseload (~100 sessions/mo) → ~$1,000/mo in client-side fees per therapist — the highest client-side take in the category (Thrizer $140–700; Reimbursify ~$279 at $3.99/claim or $99 flat). Superbilled's math: 20 weekly clients × $200 ≈ $860/mo. The benefits checker adds $348–468/yr per practice. Costs: clearinghouse (~$0.25–0.50/claim), concierge labor for denials/registrations (the real COGS), refund liability on rejected claims. Revenue is fully volume-linked to *claims filed*, not outcomes, which is why the 5% is charged pre-deductible.

## STRENGTHS
1. Cleanest therapist-side pitch in the category: $0, no contract, no payment-rail switch, keep your EHR. This removes both of Thrizer's objections (forced processor move, 3% provider fee) and Reimbursify's ($59–99/mo practice tier). It is why practices publish their own "Mentaya FAQ" pages for clients (Hello Calm, McNulty Counseling, Simplify Atlanta) — free co-marketing.
2. Zero client effort. Session confirmation by text is the lowest-friction claim trigger anyone has shipped; the client never sees a superbill. Their line — Reimbursify "doesn't reduce the administrative burden that causes most clients to give up on reimbursement" — is accurate and is the emotional core of the product.
3. Concierge + fee-refund guarantee. Denials, resubmissions, and non-par registrations are handled by humans at no charge, and a rejected claim costs the client nothing. Thrizer explicitly declines to guarantee outcomes; Reimbursify has no follow-up function at all.
4. The benefits checker is a genuine acquisition tool for therapists (VOB before first session, embeddable on the website) and a recurring-revenue line that does not depend on claim volume. It also gives Mentaya a reason to be installed on practices that never file a claim.
5. Distribution machine built on the therapist podcast/CE circuit: Practice of the Practice, Testing Psychologist, Group Practice Exchange, Private Practice Elevation, Trauma Therapist, Productive Therapist, plus the Heard partnership (therapist accountants) — each with a promo code. Cheap CAC, high trust.
6. Free EHR in beta. Even a thin one lets a solo private-pay therapist run their whole practice on Mentaya at $0, which makes the 5% client fee the only revenue line and turns Mentaya into a SimplePractice substitute for the OON segment.
7. VC-backed (Pear, Banana) with a repeatable, no-float, no-lending model — no balance-sheet risk, unlike Thrizer's Instant Reimbursement book.

## WEAKNESSES
1. Most expensive option for clients, and charged on every session including pre-deductible ones where the client gets $0 back. Thrizer calls it "the most expensive option for clients"; a client on a $3,000 OON deductible pays Mentaya ~$150 before seeing a dime. No coinsurance-only or instant-reimbursement lever to offset it.
2. The guarantee is narrower than the marketing. "Successful reimbursement or your money back" (site) vs. "if we don't successfully get your claim submitted" (help center). A claim that is accepted and then paid at $0 toward deductible, or denied on medical-necessity grounds after appeal, may not qualify — a trust-erosion risk once clients read the fine print.
3. Therapist-gated. There is no client-only path: if the therapist isn't on Mentaya, the client cannot use it at all. That leaves the patient-direct lane (now vacated by Thrizer) entirely to Reimbursify and to Ryan.
4. Thin product surface: no native mobile app, no SimplePractice/TherapyNotes/Jane integrations (the "works with your EHR" claim is coexistence, not sync), EHR is beta and by-email access only, no add-on CPT codes, no paper-trail/claim-status visibility documented beyond a portal. Group practices still need a human to click confirm per session per clinician.
5. Zero public scale signals and zero third-party reviews (Slashdot: none; no Capterra/G2 presence found). Pre-seed, 1–10 employees per Crunchbase, three named people on the About page. Claims of "an entire team of insurance pros" and "24/7 support" are not reconcilable with that headcount without offshore or contractor labor.
6. Leadership transition. The founder has stepped back to President/Chairman and a non-technical CEO from the psychology side has taken over — could be a growth move or a signal the founder is stepping away; either way it is unannounced and adds key-person uncertainty.
7. Payer exclusions match everyone else's (no HMO/Medicaid/Medicare) but Mentaya also stays inside mental health, so specialty breadth is narrower than Reimbursify's PT/dietitian/SLP/fertility coverage.
8. Revenue is indexed to claim *volume*, not client *outcomes*, and concierge cost is indexed to *denials*. As payers tighten OON, Mentaya's COGS rise while its revenue per claim is fixed — a margin squeeze with no pricing lever short of raising the 5%, which is already the category ceiling.

## OPPORTUNITIES
1. Own the private-pay therapist stack. Free EHR + free claims + $29 VOB is a bundle SimplePractice ($49–99/mo) can't match on price for the ~34% of psychologists who take no insurance. If the EHR gets to parity on notes/telehealth/intake, Mentaya becomes the default OS for OON practices.
2. Instant/ERA-driven claim status and reconciliation. They have the concierge team but no visible automation; productizing 276/277 status and ERA ingestion would cut COGS and let them finally publish the metrics (days-to-pay, denial rate, appeal win rate) that would settle the category.
3. Adjacent specialties they already say they want: SLP, PT/OT, dietitians, functional medicine — all superbill-heavy, all outside Headway/Alma. Their text-confirmation model ports cleanly.
4. Group-practice / RCM tier. Admin roles exist; a managed OON billing service for 10–50-clinician groups priced against human billers (5–8% of collections) would be a step up from client-paid 5%.
5. Pricing flexibility they haven't used: cap the 5% in dollars, waive pre-deductible fees, or offer a therapist-absorbed plan (Thrizer notes therapists can absorb the 5%). Any of these would neutralize the "most expensive for clients" critique overnight.
6. Same macro tailwinds as the rest of the category: private-pay migration, $157 vs. $111 private/in-network rate gap, HDHP growth making VOB a sales tool, DOL keeping network adequacy as an MHPAEA enforcement priority (FAB 2026-03).
7. Data asset: every claim they file produces payer × plan × CPT × allowed-amount data that would make the benefits checker a real predictor rather than an estimate — and a sellable dataset to EHRs.

## THREATS
1. EHRs absorb the feature. SimplePractice already files OON courtesy claims electronically for free; if it adds text-confirmation and a client VOB widget, Mentaya's "free to therapist" advantage evaporates and its own beta EHR is years behind.
2. A cheaper, outcome-priced competitor. Anyone charging a flat $8–15/claim *with* follow-up automation, or a fee only on reimbursed dollars, makes 5%-on-everything look predatory. Reimbursify already anchors the low end at $3.99; Thrizer's 1% direct-deposit tier post-deductible is 5× cheaper for the same claim.
3. Patient-direct AI entrants (Ryan's lane). Mentaya cannot serve a patient whose therapist isn't enrolled; a product that extracts any superbill and runs the follow-up loop takes the long tail of clients Mentaya's therapist-gated model structurally can't reach, and can then offer therapists the same "confirm by text" convenience without gating.
4. Payer hardening: non-par registration mandates, paper-check-only (Anthem), reference-based OON pricing, AI-driven denials. Each one raises Mentaya's concierge cost per claim while the fee stays at 5%.
5. Regulatory: DOL's Sept 2026 non-enforcement of the 2024 parity rule's data provisions eases pressure on plans to improve OON reimbursement; state rules on third-party claim submitters and on percentage-of-charge billing arrangements are latent risks for a client-paid percentage model.
6. Trust and PHI exposure for a ~10-person team with no disclosed SOC 2, running text-based PHI workflows (SMS session confirmations carry diagnosis-linked data). One incident in the therapist Facebook groups where their reputation lives would be disproportionately damaging.
7. Capital and leadership risk: pre-seed only, no follow-on round found, founder moved to Chairman. If the next raise doesn't come, the free EHR and human concierge are the first things to be cut — and both are the moat.

## Implications for Ryan's superbill-claims product
- Mentaya is the *therapist-distributed* incumbent; it is not a direct competitor for a patient who arrives with a superbill from a non-enrolled provider. That patient is your user and Mentaya cannot touch them. Don't build a therapist portal first; build the patient loop, then let therapists opt in (Mentaya's text-confirm trick is worth copying as a *provider-side accelerator* later).
- Their pricing is the anchor to undercut on fairness, not on price: 5% on every session, including pre-deductible, is the single most-cited complaint. A flat per-claim fee or a monthly subscription that explicitly does not charge when the claim only feeds the deductible is a clean positioning line ("we don't charge you to get $0 back").
- Their guarantee gap is your guarantee. Make yours about the *loop* — "we chase it until it's adjudicated, and you can see every status change" — rather than a fee refund on rejections. Publish the metrics they don't (days to pay, denial rate, appeal win rate).
- Match the concierge with automation, not headcount: 276/277 status polling, ERA/EOB parsing, denial-reason explanation, templated appeals and regulator complaints. Mentaya's "entire team of insurance pros" is their biggest cost line and your biggest cost advantage.
- Copy the benefits checker as a free onboarding step (270/271 eligibility) — it is the highest-value moment in their funnel, and they charge therapists $29–39/mo for it.
- Scope exclusions are identical (PPO/POS/HDHP only; no HMO/Medicaid/Medicare) — bake them into onboarding. Support add-on CPT codes from day one; it is a documented Mentaya gap that loses them testing psychologists and will lose them PT/OT.
- Watch two signals: whether Mentaya ships a client-only intake (they'd be moving into your lane) and whether the free EHR leaves beta (they'd be becoming a practice OS, which makes them less of a claims competitor and more of a distribution partner target).
